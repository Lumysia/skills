# English Humanization Rules

These rules cover English and mostly English prose.

Treat patterns as clusters, not automatic errors. A single pattern can be normal; stacked patterns often create the generic AI feel.

## Structure Patterns

- Formulaic openings that say the topic is important before saying anything specific.
- Conclusions that restate the prompt, become upbeat, or promise future potential without new information.
- Overbalanced sections that force pros, cons, challenges, and future outlook when the genre does not need them.
- Lists with identical item shape, bold labels, and colon summaries.
- Headings followed by warm-up sentences that merely repeat the heading.
- Diff-anchored prose outside changelogs, release notes, or migration guides.

## Language Patterns

- Significance inflation: pivotal, crucial, transformative, testament, landscape, underscores, showcases.
- Vague authorities: experts say, observers note, industry reports suggest, according to sources.
- Empty transitions: additionally, moreover, furthermore, in conclusion, let's explore.
- Forced triads and false ranges: three-item symmetry or `from X to Y` when no real scale exists.
- Copula avoidance: `serves as`, `stands as`, `boasts`, `features` when `is` or `has` is clearer.
- Hedging piles: could potentially possibly, it may be argued, it is important to note.
- Superficial `-ing` tails that add fake analysis: highlighting, underscoring, showcasing, reflecting.
- Synonym cycling that avoids repeating the clearest noun.
- Negative parallelism overuse: `not only X but Y`, `not just about X, it is about Y`.

## Content Patterns

- Abstract claims with no example, source, mechanism, or consequence.
- Promotional adjectives where plain description is better: vibrant, rich, breathtaking, must-visit, groundbreaking.
- Unsupported speculation about motives, private history, impact, or future outcomes.
- Smooth but low-information paragraphs that could fit many topics.
- Knowledge-cutoff or chatbot disclaimers left inside content.

## Style And Formatting Patterns

- Em dash or en dash overuse, especially when every aside uses the same punctuation rhythm.
- Mechanical boldface, emoji labels, title-case headings, and inline-header bullet lists.
- Curly quotes or straight quotes are not signals by themselves; preserve the target publication's convention.
- Fragmented headers: a heading followed by a one-line paragraph that just repeats the heading.
- Markdown artifacts left in plain-text output.

## Rhythm Patterns

- Sentences all landing at the same length.
- Every paragraph following the same explanation pattern.
- Over-polished neutrality in genres where a human stance is expected.
- Artificial messiness added where the genre requires precision.

## Preserve

Preserve specific details, defensible opinions, mixed feelings, source-backed claims, technical terms, and genre-required neutrality.

Do not flatten expert, legal, academic, or reference prose just because it is polished.
